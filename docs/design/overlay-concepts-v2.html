Ten more ways for a voice
to glow at the edge.

Second round of animated directions for the Buddadawg dictation overlay, rebuilt around the owner’s chosen axes: voice art (the voice made visible), ambient minimal (barely-there light), and instrument (precise, calibrated motion). Grayscale throughout; a single warm accent marks the pasted state. Every preview is live and driven by the same simulated voice as round one.

Round one explored abstract grammars (shorelines, terrain, seismographs) and was rejected wholesale. This round hews to three axes the owner picked: voice art — the voice made visible as ribbon, signature or waveform; ambient minimal — barely-there fields of light; instrument — precise calibrated motion like a scope, gauge or comb. All ten share the same simulated voice and state controls as round one. Click any desktop to inspect it large.

Ground rules (from the code)

  • Panel: full screen width × 240 pt, glow at zero by 221 pt — no visible edge, ever.
  • One fullscreen triangle, half-res, 120 Hz, renders only while visible; fails dark.
  • Signals: level (rise 20/s, fall 6/s), swell (7/s, 3.5/s), integrated voice-warped phases.
  • Four states crossfade by weight: listening, transcribing, pasted (0.62 s), cancelled (0.20 s).
  • Colour rule for this round: grayscale normally; a warm amber accent appears only in the pasted state — the moment the dictation lands.
  • No text, icons, bars or meters in the overlay. No springs, no overshoot. Reduced Motion honoured.

Recommendation

Top three, ranked

Scored for: unique & ownable · peripheral calm · OG-compatible (grayscale, bottom-edge, grain-friendly) · implementable inside today’s one-triangle shader and PhaseClock without new passes or buffers.

  1. 1

    02Signature — every dictation leaves a mark

    The most ownable idea on the page: the phrase literally draws itself as an abstract pen-stroke along the bottom edge — pressure and wander driven by the voice, so no two dictations look alike. It turns the overlay from a status light into a receipt: you can see the shape of what you just said. Cheap to render (a polyline), impossible to confuse with any other app, and the warm accent on paste makes the completed signature feel signed and done.

  2. 2

    01Silk Ribbon — voice as liquid light

    The most beautiful literal read of the voice: layered flowing ribbons whose undulation is the envelope, silk rather than waveform. It keeps Grain Horizon’s additive-light grammar but replaces peaks-and-grain with continuous cloth-like motion — quieter in peripheral vision, richer up close. Moderate shader cost (a few summed sines per pixel, same pass).

  3. 3

    04Pilot Lamp — one breathing light

    The calmest option: a single soft lamp at bottom-centre that swells with the voice, heartbeats while transcribing, and flashes warm when the text lands. Almost zero peripheral distraction, trivially cheap, and the pasted ring gives the accent somewhere meaningful to live. Its risk is thinness — it may read as “just a glow” unless the breathing is tuned beautifully.

Watch: 05 Scope Trail and 08 Sonar Rings are the strongest instrument entries but trail-buffer history is new state the shader doesn’t have today — they need a small ring buffer or a second pass. 06 VU Arc and 07 Frequency Comb are pure fragment-shader swaps (cheap) but flirt with the “no meters” rule; judge whether they read as instruments or gauges. 03 Aurora Veil and 10 Mist Fields are the ambient pair — safest, subtlest, and the most likely to be called “same as before but blurrier.”

How to read these previews

Each desktop is a scale fiction: the overlay band stands in for the real 240 pt panel, drawn over menu bar, document and Dock exactly as the status-bar-level panel composites in the product. Brightness here is illustrative — the shipping shader caps coverage at alpha = G × .85, premultiplied, so the wallpaper always shows through.

The simulated voice is shared by all ten previews: a phrase gate (2–4 s of speech, 0.6–1.4 s of pause) multiplied by syllabic bursts near 4–7 Hz, then smoothed with the app’s constants — level rises at 20/s and falls at 6/s; swell at 7/s and 3.5/s. Animation phases are integrated (shaftTime += dt × (1 + 1.5 level)), never sin(elapsed × f(level)), mirroring BarkCore.PhaseClock.

Implementation sketches assume today’s contract unless stated: one fullscreen triangle; uniforms res, dpr, t, lvl, show, ph, swell, tShaft, tBump, reduce, s[4]; half-res drawable; grayscale intensity G computed first and returned premultiplied. “No new uniforms” means a concept can ship as a shader-body swap behind the existing show(_:) / level interface.

  • Difficulty 1 = shader-body swap · 3 = new CPU signal or instancing · 5 = new render architecture.
  • Distraction risk judges motion in peripheral vision at 60–120 cm, mid-sentence, eyes on text.
  • Reduced Motion (system setting or the toggle above) damps decorative phase advance to 15%, as in the app; state timing stays real-time.
