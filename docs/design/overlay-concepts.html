Ten ways for a voice
to glow at the edge.

Animated directions for the Buddadawg dictation overlay — judged on peripheral calm, ownability, and fit with the shipping Metal architecture. Every preview on this page is live and driven by the same simulated voice.

The shipping overlay is Grain Horizon: a full-width, 240 pt, click-through panel along the bottom screen edge, rendered by a single Metal fragment shader at half resolution. Light shafts sway behind seven voice-driven peaks; 24-step film grain keeps it photographic rather than digital. It appears here as Concept 01, the baseline — the control every other direction has to beat, not merely differ from.

The nine challengers change the grammar, not the paint: a shoreline, a seismograph drum, contour lines, an eclipse limb, rising ash, proof-marks, ridgelines, ink blooms, a reed bed. All ten share one simulated voice (phrase envelope × syllabic bursts, smoothed with the app’s own constants) and one state control below, so differences you see are differences of idea, not of input. Click any desktop to inspect it large.

Ground rules (from the code)

  • Panel: full screen width × 240 pt, glow at zero by 221 pt — no visible edge, ever.
  • One fullscreen triangle, half-res, 120 Hz, renders only while visible; fails dark.
  • Signals: level (rise 20/s, fall 6/s), swell (7/s, 3.5/s), integrated voice-warped phases.
  • Four states crossfade by weight: listening, transcribing, pasted (0.62 s), cancelled (0.20 s).
  • No text, icons, bars or meters in the overlay. No springs, no overshoot. Reduced Motion honoured.
Owner decision · 12 Nov 2026 · binding on this page

The butter-tinted glow was rejected on screen; the shipping overlay stays original grayscale. Accordingly, nine concepts here are strictly monochrome. Exactly one (10 · Reed Bed) shows butter as a single 3 px tick for under a second, behind a toggle, labelled optional — included only so it can be judged and, by default in product, deleted.

Recommendation

Top three, ranked

Scored for: unique & ownable · peripheral calm · OG-compatible (grayscale, bottom-edge, grain-friendly) · implementable inside today’s one-triangle shader and PhaseClock without new passes or buffers.

  1. 1

    04Contour — topographic voice terrain

    The strongest successor. It reuses the existing bumps() peaks verbatim as a heightfield and draws elevation lines instead of filled light, so it is a re-expression of Grain Horizon’s own mathematics — same uniforms, same phase warping, same pass. Lines carry less luminous area than a glow, so it sits quieter in peripheral vision, yet “terrain rising as you speak” is a metaphor no dictation product owns. It also degrades beautifully: at silence it is a near-flat map, not an empty rectangle.

  2. 2

    05Eclipse Limb — one great arc

    The calmest and cheapest idea on the page: a single signed-distance circle whose bright limb breaches the screen edge. Unmistakable at a glance, almost still at rest, and trivially within budget. It ranks second only because its state vocabulary is thinner — transcribing must narrow the arc rather than change character — and the dark disc needs careful alpha over light wallpapers, where Grain Horizon’s additive glow is more forgiving.

  3. 3

    02Tide Line — a shoreline with a memory

    The most peripheral direction here: one waterline, plus fading high-water marks that remember how loud the phrase was. Nearly zero distraction and a one-line SDF in the shader, needing only two extra uniforms for stored peak heights. Its risk is legibility of the wrong kind — too crisp and it reads as a level meter, which the visual language forbids. Kept grainy and rippled, it stays a shoreline; that discipline is the whole concept.

Hold: 09 Ink Bloom and 06 Rising Ash are lovely but need a feedback target or a particle pass — a new architecture, not a new look; revisit only if the overlay ever grows a second pass for other reasons. 07 Underline needs an explicit owner ruling against the “no text in the overlay, ever” rule before any prototyping. And the honest control remains 01: if none of the three above beats it on a real screen, over real wallpapers, Grain Horizon should simply keep shipping.

How to read these previews

Each desktop is a scale fiction: the overlay band stands in for the real 240 pt panel, drawn over menu bar, document and Dock exactly as the status-bar-level panel composites in the product. Brightness here is illustrative — the shipping shader caps coverage at alpha = G × .85, premultiplied, so the wallpaper always shows through.

The simulated voice is shared by all ten previews: a phrase gate (2–4 s of speech, 0.6–1.4 s of pause) multiplied by syllabic bursts near 4–7 Hz, then smoothed with the app’s constants — level rises at 20/s and falls at 6/s; swell at 7/s and 3.5/s. Animation phases are integrated (shaftTime += dt × (1 + 1.5 level)), never sin(elapsed × f(level)), mirroring BarkCore.PhaseClock.

Implementation sketches assume today’s contract unless stated: one fullscreen triangle; uniforms res, dpr, t, lvl, show, ph, swell, tShaft, tBump, reduce, s[4]; half-res drawable; grayscale intensity G computed first and returned premultiplied. “No new uniforms” means a concept can ship as a shader-body swap behind the existing show(_:) / level interface.

  • Difficulty 1 = shader-body swap · 3 = new CPU signal or instancing · 5 = new render architecture.
  • Distraction risk judges motion in peripheral vision at 60–120 cm, mid-sentence, eyes on text.
  • Reduced Motion (system setting or the toggle above) damps decorative phase advance to 15%, as in the app; state timing stays real-time.
